# The Empire of Texas
## A Post-Fall Fantasy Campaign Setting

Set in a distant future Texas, this campaign blends late Medieval and early Renaissance feudalism with remnants of pre-fall technology.

### The Geopolitical Realm
* **The Empire of Texas:** A fragmented, human-dominated empire in name only—comprising rump Texas, rump Oklahoma, and western Louisiana.
* **The Empire of Mexico:** A secretive, closed empire to the south that remains largely unknown.
* **New Mexico:** A high-magic, otherworldly neighbor dominated by High Elves who hold Old West Texas and treat other species with disdain.
* **Eastern Louisiana:** A low-magic, hostile neighbor of impassable swamps and barbaric marsh clans.
* **The Oklahoma Marches:** Former Indian territories functioning as a shamanistic region under no regional power.
* **Borderlands & Independent Powers:** Free cities like Norlens or El Vado under city-state rule.
* **Distant Powers & Trade Partners:** Kanzas and Missoura to the north; Yucata and Havana across the Gulf; strange metals from the Iron Reach.
## Key Landscape Features

### Balcones Escarpment (The Shield)
The dramatic elevation jump along the Balcones Fault creates a natural stone rampart through the heart of the state. Sa'atone sits right at this drop-off. In a feudal setting, this escarpment is the Great Wall of the West, allowing Texas to easily defend the fertile lowlands against Elven rangers.

### Artesian Spring Belt (The Belt)
The fault line unleashes the Edwards Aquifer in massive, pressurized springs—Comal, San Marcos, Barton, and San Pedro. Controlling these high-volume springs means controlling all regional trade, agriculture, and water power for millwheels.

### Llano Uplift & Enchanted Rock
A billion-year-old granite batholith north of Sa'atone rich in iron ore and rare earth minerals. This is the prime Artificer Crucible of Texas—a region of massive pink stone fortresses and ancient domes housing pre-fall tech.

### Coastal Salt Domes (The Deep Vaults)
Subsurface salt domes near the Gulf coast. Hollowed-out salt domes make dry, virtually indestructible subterranean vaults for storing pre-fall machinery, black powder reserves, or contraband.

### The Caprock Escarpment
A sheer, 1,000-foot wall separating the rolling plains from the High Plains. It serves as a natural border separating human Texan fiefs from nomadic shamanistic territories.

### Big Bend
The Chisos Mountains remain a Texan redoubt surrounded by Elven West Texas, relying on secret mountain paths and trade routes with Mexican smugglers to survive.

### The Trans-Pecos Mountain Sanctuaries
The Davis and Guadalupe mountains act as high-altitude extensions of the Rockies held by New Mexico as high-magic strongholds commanding the desert floor.

### Western River Borders
The Pecos River forms a natural moat against the Elven West. The Rio Grand separates the Empire of Mexico from Texas and New Mexico.

### The Marches & Piney Woods
To the east, the Ouachita Mountains and Piney Woods form a natural border. Western Louisiana sits on drier timberland easily ruled by Texan knights.

### The Moat (Atchafalaya)
The true eastern barrier is the Atchafalaya Basin. Eastern Louisiana decays into an impassable maze of toxic bayous where heavily armored armies drown.

## Settlements of the Realm

### Core Fiefdoms of Texas

#### Ostin (formerly Austin)
Perched on the Balcones Escarpment straddling the Colorado River. The Imperial Capital. The Grand Council and the Emperor sit in the Pink Citadel—a massive fortress constructed from Llano granite.

#### Metro (formerly Dallas & Fort Worth)
Twin rival strongholds along the Trinity River. Futworth is a stockade fortress of heavy cavalry and cattle barons; Dallas is a wealthy trade city built among skeletal skyscraper frames.

#### The Ring-Cities of Houston (The Beltway Barons)
Fortified, bridge-mounted city-states suspended on the elevated flyovers of Loop 610 and Beltway 8 above a flooded bayou. Beneath lies Grave City—the toxic, submerged ruins explored by salvage divers.

#### Iron-Dome (Llano / Enchanted Rock)
A smoke-choked fortress city built into pink granite batholiths. Powered by geothermal vents, its blast furnaces forge black-powder cannons and refined alloys.

#### Shreve Fort (formerly Shreveport)
A heavy timber-and-stone bastion in the Piney Woods guarding the eastern frontier against swamp incursions across the Atchafalaya.

#### Viktoria (formerly Victoria)
A fortified prairie stronghold along the Guadalupe River serving as the Empire's southern staging ground, cattle hub, and trade junction.

#### Port Corpus (formerly Corpus Christi)
A heavily fortified naval harbor on Corpus Christi Bay protected by Padre Island forts. Builds armed coastal galleys and guards sea lanes.

#### Beeville
The southernmost militarized outpost before the lawless Brush Marches. Built on an ancient airfield, it serves as a war-engine depot supplied with pitch from Three Rivers.

#### Boca Chica ("The Star-Gantries")
A contested coastal enclave towering over salt marshes. Launch gantries and silos hold pristine aerospace alloys, warded by lethal pre-fall automated drones.

### Interior & Frontier Towns

#### Wako (formerly Waco)
Situated at the Brazos River crossing along the Balcones corridor. Famous for the Suspension Gate—a pre-times cable bridge re-engineered into a fortified toll-bridge.

#### Nacog (formerly Nacogdoches)
Hidden deep within the Piney Woods along the Camino Real. A timber-and-stone sanctuary town of moss-draped abbeys and woodsmen serving as a key trade stop.

#### Spindle-Bay (formerly Beaumont / Port Arthur)
A soot-choked refinery port where petroleum naturally seeps. Converts crude oil into pitch and incendiary fuel for naval galleys and siege engines.

#### Frederik (formerly Fredericksburg)
Nestled in a fertile Hill Country valley west of Ostin. An insular fiefdom of stonecutters and master brewers whose limestone walls defend against Elven rangers.

#### San Anjel (formerly San Angelo)
A fortified oasis town at the junction of the Concho River branches. Famous for freshwater pearls and deep wells, serving as a key desert staging post.

#### The Salt Lands

The realm runs on a mix of coin and direct barter. Salt is a high-value export heavily protected. Key sources include:

* **El Sal del Rey (Ed'nburg / lower valley):** A lake north of the Rio Grand where salt chrystalizes on the shoreline, and harvesting is heavily guarded.
* **San Elizario Flats (Guadaloop Mountains):** Ancient alkali salt beds lost to New Mexico. A source of conflict.
* **Grand Saline (formerly Van Zandt County):** An easily accessed source of mineral salt. Imperial miners call it *Morton's Salt* after an umbrella emblem is still etched into shafts.
* **Hockley Vaults (Houston Region):** Dangerous underground pillars of rock salt near *Grave City*. Rich in salt, lower shafts are poisoned from old times and need special gear.
* **The Salt Forks (North-Central Marches):** Salterns in briny rivers including the Red River Salt Fork, the Brazos Salt Fork, and Salt Croton Creek. Some may evaporate salt in pans, occasionally finding also fossils.

### Borderlands & Strongholds

#### El Vado (formerly Laredo)
An independent Free City sitting astride the narrowest low-water crossing of the Rio Grand, ruled by a council of merchant-smugglers.

#### Browns Delta
A stilted boomtown near the mouth of the Rio Grand. A lawless haven for scavengers, river pirates, and pilgrims, governed informally by Las Hermandades del Río.

#### The Chisos Citadel (Big Bend Redoubt)
An isolated volcanic bowl accessible only through narrow mountain passes like The Window. A forward Texan enclave relying on river smugglers.

#### El Pazo (formerly El Paso)
Controlled by the Sun-Elves of New Mexico. Built with white stone and solar-glass spires commanding the Franklin Mountain pass.

#### The Guadalupe Spires
An arcane watchtower complex carved into the sheer limestone cliffs of El Capitan, garrisoned by Elven rangers and griffon-riders.

## Independent Powers & Distant Trade

#### Redwater (The Red River Market)
A sprawling boomtown built on wooden stilts along the Red River corridor, serving as the primary neutral trading post with nomadic tribal confederacies.

#### Norlens (formerly New Orleans)
An independent, canal-riddled merchant republic ruled by river-guilds and privateers. Defended by sea-walls and mercenary fleets.

#### Iron Reach (formerly Cincinnati)
Heavy iron barges navigate down the Ohio and Miss'ippi rivers through Norlens (where cargo is heavily taxed), bringing coal, iron castings, copper, and whiskey.

#### The Rockies (formerly Colorado & Wyoming)
Long-distance armed wagon trains travel north through Kansas before turning west along the Arkansas River, bringing gold, raw silver, gemstones, pelts, and pine resin.

#### The Ozarks (formerly Arkansas)
A wild, lawless hill region shunned by formal imperial armies. Traders barter Texan salt and refined oil for lead, zinc, timber, medicinal herbs, and stone.

#### The Empire of Mexico
Secretive southern realm. Smugglers passing the Camino Real through El Vado carry silver coin, cochineal dyes, fine leatherwork, and obsidian in exchange for horses and pre-times machinery.

## Orders, Guilds & Societies

* **The Derrick League (Black-Tar Guild):** Deep-well artificers and refinery barons who control crude oil extraction in Houston and Spindle-Bay.
* **The Range-Keepers (Star-Badge Order):** Mounted lawmen and scouts wearing silver star-badges who enforce the "Law of the Prairie" across open trade roads.
* **Las Hermandades del Río:** Family-led smuggling networks operating along the Rio Grand, moving black-market star-relics and refugees.
* **The Star-Chamber Guild:** Academic artificers obsessed with aerospace salvage in Boca Chica, offering bounties for ancient guidance drives and glass optical disks.

## Pilgrimages

Protected by old times holy traditions, pilgrams serve a diplomatic role between Texas and neighboring realms. These succor both the traveler and provide unofficial means to diplomacy.

{{note
##### Mechanical Rule: The Pilgrim Badge
Pilgrims wear stamped metal badges on their garment reflecting their destination. Openly wearing a known badge grants advantage to pass border checkpoints and tollgates without being turned back or paying unusual fees. Those discovered as not pilgrams suffer more severe penalties.
}}

### Common Sites

* **The High Shrine of El Capitan (New Mexico):** High Elves permit human pilgrims three weeks during the Autumn Equinox to walk the Camino Sun-Path to a cliff-top chapel.
* **Our Lady of Topo Chico (Mexican Border):** A coastal monastery at a rusted pre-fall bottling plant and rocket tower. Pilgrims offer fragments of ancient green glass.
* **The Basilica of Prompt Succor (Norlens):** A sea-walled stone cathedral where river traders make the Great River Vow for safe passage through swamp monster waters.
* **Medicine Mounts of Wichita (Oklahoma):** Iron-rich granite peaks operating under the Truce of the Red Earth—a weapon-free zone to negotiate treaties.
* **Our Lady of Guadalupe (Mexico Interior):** Houses the Star-Mantle—a cloak of indestructible pre-fall fiber with glowing constellation threads.

## Starter Hub: Sa'atone

Perched at the base of the Balcones Escarpment, **Sa'atone** (San Antonio) is a bustling oasis city dominating river trade, grain production, and southern defense.

### Key City Districts

1. **The Stonecut Market (The River Cut):** A limestone canyon carved by the river, shaded by collapsed concrete highway flyovers, featuring *The Glass Barge* tavern.
2. **The Alamo Citadel:** A stone fortress clad in scavenged armor plates, serving as the monastery of **The Texas Ranger Guard**.
3. **The Aquifer Vaults:** 150-foot-deep subterranean flood tunnels run by **The Order of the Wellspring** to control pumps and sluice gates.
4. **The Mission Fiefdoms:** Fortified agricultural manors (San José, Espada) ruled by **Acequia Barons** who control irrigation canals.
5. **The Sun-Crag:** The elevated limestone ridge above the gorge where wealthy merchants and officers reside.

### Prominent Local NPCs
* **Grand Marshal Valeria Vance:** Grim commander of the Texas Ranger Guard at the Alamo.
* **High Hydromancer Hector Cruz:** Reclusive leader of the Order of the Wellspring.
* **Baroness Ysabel de Espada:** Ambitious ruler of Mission Espada.
* **"Gutter-Jack" Reyes:** Affable chief of the River Cut Smugglers' Guild.

{{descriptive
##### Local Laws & Customs
* **Law of the Pint:** Stealing or contaminating drinking water is a capital offense. Public drinking fountains are free to all.
* **The Noon-Gloom:** Midday in the River Cut feels like dusk. Vendors use copper mirrors on the canyon rim to reflect light down.
* **Flash-Flood Alarm:** Iron gongs warn of mountain rain. Stalls are hoisted up cliff terraces before floodwaters surge.
}}

### Sa'atone Adventure Seeds

* **The Stolen Sluice-Key:** A rogue artificer has sabotaged the primary pressure release valve in the Aquifer Vaults, threatening to flood the River Cut unless a rare replacement gear is retrieved from a drowning Houston vault.
* **The Smuggler's Badge:** "Gutter-Jack" needs an escort for a caravan of pilgrims carrying forged Topo Chico badges through the Range-Keeper checkpoints outside Beeville.
* **Desert Relic Run:** Baroness Ysabel hires the party to trek past the Chisos Redoubt into Mexican borderlands to recover a pre-fall solar irrigation module from an abandoned hacienda.

## Currency & Barter

{{descriptive
##### Quick Conversion Reference
* **10 Brass Bits** = 1 Silver Star
* **10 Silver Stars** = 1 Golden Spur
* **10 Golden Spurs** = 1 Cotton Scrip (10 GP)
* **Pre-Fall Scrap:** Unrefined copper wire, intact glass bottles, and sealed spark-cells are accepted as direct barter in most border markets.
}}

### Empire of Texas

#### Brass Bit (1 CP Equivalent)
Rough octagonal discs stamped with a crude Imperial Star serving for most common simple exchange. Sometimes called "Shells" or "Bits". Brass is still made using ancient methods predating even old times.

#### Silver Star or "Lone Star" (1 SP Equivalent)
A round silver coin minted in Ostin and the banker-nobles of Dallas. Well-stamped with the Lone Star on the obverse and the Balcones Escarpment on the reverse, it is the workhorse currency for common market trade, inn stays, and soldier pay.

#### Golden Spur or "Pink Crown" (1 GP Equivalent)
A heavy, round gold coin minted in Ostin alone from gold extracted from the Llano Uplift batholith. Well-stamped with an ornate Lone Star on the obverse and the Pink Citadel on the reverse, legal for landowners, merchants, and senior officers of the Texas Ranger Guard to carry.

#### Cotton Scrip / Vault Notes (10 GP / 1 PP Equivalent)
Water-resistant banknotes printed on heavy woven cotton-rag paper and sealed with a wax-and-lead imprint from the Banker-Nobles of Dallas or the Salt Vaults of Houston. Often viewed with suspicion in rural fiefs, Cotton Scrip acts as a paper letter of credit redeemable for physical bullion or salt rights at major banking houses.

#### Black Gold (Barrel Reserve / 500 GP Equivalent)
A standard 42-gallon sealed barrel of refined crude or petroleum pitch. Too heavy for daily market transactions, barrels of Black Gold are hoarded by the Derrick League and used to settle major inter-fief debts, purchase war-engines, or fund military campaigns.

### Other Realms

* **Mexican Silver Reales:** High-purity, heavy silver coins minted by the secretive Empire of Mexico. Highly valued by Texan merchants and smugglers for their reliable weight, often fetching a 10–20% premium over Imperial Stars.
* **Elven Solar-Glass Tokens:** Blown from high-purity silica glass and enchanted by the High Elves of New Mexico. They glow with a faint, warm sunlight when held and cannot be forged by outside smiths. Artificers in Texas pay steep prices to study their permanent luminescence.
* **Dead-Paper (Pre-Fall Currency):** Preserved pre-fall federal banknotes. Merchants and innkeepers reject them outright as useless clutter, though hedge-alchemists and blacksmiths occasionally buy bundles for a single Brass Bit to use as kindling or pipe-wadding. Some believe this paper has powers to discover.
